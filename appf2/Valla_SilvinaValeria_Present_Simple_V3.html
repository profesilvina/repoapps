Present Simple Practice

Accesibilidad:

Use

Present Simple is used to talk about habits (I brush my teeth every day) and routines (I go to school in the morning).

Form: affirmative and negative

I / You / We / They      verb=infinitive (it does not change, it stays the same. E.g. play-play)
I play football on Monday.
don’t/ do not + verb in the infinitive
I don’t go to the club on Sunday.

He / She / It      verb+-s
She dances every day.
verb+-es (verbs ending in -z, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x, -o (preceded by a consonant)
E.g. whiz-whizzes, possess-possesses, wash-washes, teach-teaches, mix-mixes, go-goes.
Marcos does homework at four.
Lara washes her clothes at the weekend.

Verbs ending in a consonant+-y
Change –y to -i and add –es. E.g. study- studies
She studies English on Monday.

doesn’t/does not+verb in the infinitive
Juan doesn’t study Maths on Saturday.

Form: interrogative

Auxiliary verbs

I / You / We / They      do
He / She / It      does

Questions are formed like this:
An auxiliary verb + a subject + a verb + …?

Do you go to school at seven? Yes, I/we do. / No, I/we don’t.
Does he/she play tennis on Saturday? Yes, he/she does. / No, he/she doesn’t.
Do they do homework in the evening? Yes, they do. / No, they don’t.

A wh-word + an auxiliary verb + a subject + a verb + …?
What do you do on Monday? I study Geography.
Where do you play football? I play football at the club.
When do you visit your friends? I visit my friends on Saturday.
Who cooks dinner on Monday? My father does. / My parents do.

Adverbs of frequency

Always100%
Usually80%
Often60%
Sometimes30%
Never0%

Pay attention to their position in the sentence:

Verb to be + adverb (Verb to be is an exception)
I am always busy.

Adverb + verb
I usually get up at seven.

Time expressions

Days of the week: on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday, at the weekend (Saturday and Sunday).

Moments of the day: in the morning, in the afternoon, in the evening, at night.

Time:
at four / at four o’clock
at quarter past seven
at half past five
at quarter to eight
